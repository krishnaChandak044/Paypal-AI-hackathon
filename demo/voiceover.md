# ReliefRail demo voiceover

## Scene 01

After a disaster, help arrives from many directions. The hard part is making every decision and every movement of money easy to follow.

## Scene 02

ReliefRail brings that workflow into one place. It helps a response team see what has been requested, what has been approved, and what the shared pool can actually cover.

## Scene 03

The product is designed around a simple principle: show the state of each step, and keep the decision with the people responsible for the response.

## Scene 04

Here, the coordinator reviews a synthetic request for essential supplies. Similarity signals can surface possible overlap, but they are only heuristics. They can miss a match, and they never decide eligibility or fraud.

## Scene 05

The AI contribution is practical: turn a short request into a structured draft that a coordinator can edit. ReliefRail keeps the boundary clear. The model does not rank people, decide eligibility, detect fraud, or authorize money.

## Scene 06

This is not an autonomous relief agent. AI helps with organization and explanation; authorized humans keep full control over aid decisions and spending.

## Scene 07

That distinction matters. An approved request is not the same as cash in the pool. A reservation is not a completed payout. The board makes those states visible instead of blending them into one optimistic number.

## Scene 08

This demonstrates the payment plumbing without claiming live donations. Pool credit follows a confirmed capture. A retry does not create a second ledger credit for the same provider event.

## Scene 09

If a payout is confirmed successful, the reserve becomes a spend. If it fails, the reservation is released. If the provider state is unclear, the amount stays reserved while reconciliation checks again.

## Scene 10

The payout path is intentionally described for what it is: a sandbox payout request to a test recipient. Provider status drives the ledger. Unconfirmed outcomes stay pending; they are not presented as completed aid.

## Scene 11

A receipt scan is an aid to review, not an audit certification. The coordinator checks the extracted details and reconciles them against the approved need.

## Scene 12

ReliefRail is a prototype for better coordination and accountability. It does not replace emergency responders, local organizations, or the judgment of the people doing the work.

## Scene 13

The scope is deliberate: make a complex relief workflow more reviewable, while keeping sensitive decisions with people and reporting only what the system can verify.

## Scene 14

ReliefRail turns a shared pool into a clearer path from an approved need to a confirmed outcome. Thank you.
